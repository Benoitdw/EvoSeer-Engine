## Rationale

In Approximate Bayesian Computation (ABC), each candidate parameter set θ (the vector of biological parameters — division rate, death rate, mutation rate, senescence, etc.) is evaluated by comparing a simulated distribution $F_n$ (based on n stochastic runs) to a reference distribution $R$. A sufficient number of runs per parameter set is needed to ensure that rejection of $\theta$ reflects a genuine mismatch between the model and the data — and not simply a poor Monte Carlo estimate of the underlying distribution.

Mathematically, we define $D(F_n, R)$ as a distance between the empirical distribution F_n (based on n Gillespie simulations) and the reference distribution $R$. We want to find the minimal $n$ such that the variance of $D$ is negligible — i.e. adding more simulations does not meaningfully change our estimate of the distance.

## What theory tells us

This is a well-studied problem in nonparametric statistics (CLT, DKW inequality, empirical process theory). The short answer: any distance computed from $F_n$ converges to its true value at rate $O(1/\sqrt{n})$ — doubling simulations halves the error, but with diminishing returns. More importantly, convergence hits a floor: the irreducible stochastic variance of the Gillespie process itself. Two runs with identical θ produce different trajectories — that's not estimation noise, it's biological noise. No amount of extra simulation removes it.

## The chicken-and-egg problem

To pick $n$, we need to know how noisy the simulator is at θ. But that noise depends on θ — and varies a lot: near the OIS bifurcation (e.g. BRAF V600E), trajectories become bimodal and variance spikes. A fixed $n$ calibrated on one region of parameter space may be completely wrong in another.

We can't know $n$ without running simulations, and we can't know how many simulations to run without knowing $n$.

![Convergence of distance metrics as a function of n](/EvoSeer-Engine/research-notes/sample-by-simulation-convergence.png)

All four metrics (L1, L2, KS, Wasserstein) show the same pattern: rapid drop up to n ≈ 20–50, then a plateau around n ≈ 100–150. For τ = 0.01, **n ≈ 120–150** is sufficient (see [notebook](/EvoSeer-Engine/notebooks/abc-samples/)).

## Solution: sequential stopping rule

Rather than fixing $n$ upfront, we add simulations incrementally and stop when the marginal
gain in $D(F_n, R)$ falls below a threshold:

$$\left| \frac{\Delta D}{\Delta n} \right| < \tau$$

This is the simulation equivalent of early stopping in ML — track the elbow of the convergence
curve and stop there. It adapts automatically to the local variance of the simulator, solving
the chicken-and-egg problem without any prior knowledge of θ.

## Practical recommendation

Rather than a fixed $n$, we use a **multi-stage stopping rule** that tightens the convergence
threshold progressively and can early-reject a bad θ before full convergence — saving compute.

Let $\varepsilon$ be the ABC acceptance threshold (the maximum $D$ to accept θ).

The logic: at τ = 10%, the estimate is rough but if $D$ is already 10× above the acceptance
threshold, there is no realistic path to acceptance — reject immediately. Each stage tightens
both the convergence criterion and the early-rejection bar. Only θ that survive all three
stages reach the final accept/reject decision at full precision.

Additional stages can be added by inserting `(τ, k)` pairs — the pattern generalises cleanly.
